## What changed from the previous draft, and why

The previous strategy made the **URL diagnosis** the wedge and the free report the proof. That was a defensible acquisition idea, but it under-demonstrated the vision. A beautiful report is still a report. It shows that Banyan can read a product; it does not show that Banyan can **run distribution**.

The new brief's most important recommendation is correct:

> One product. One campaign objective. One approved strategy. Three content formats. Two or three selected channels. Actual publishing. Performance feedback. A proposed next campaign.

That sequence exercises the entire system — intelligence, strategy, production, approval, publishing, measurement and learning — and produces real outcome data instead of a plausible document. It is harder to copy than a report, more convincing to a founder, and it creates the only asset that compounds: **the record of what actually worked for this product.**

**What stays from v1**

- The ICP: technical solo founders of post-launch B2B SaaS, not everyone.
- The honesty posture: approvals, provenance, real rows, honest blocked states, no fabricated integrations.
- The six-surface launch discipline: one loop, not a suite.
- The market read: schedulers execute without deciding; AI writers create without distributing; agencies do not scale down.
- The pricing shape and the customer-owned publishing account.

**What changes**

- The **completed cycle**, not the diagnosis, is the product proof.
- The MVP covers **three formats across two live channels plus one manual channel**, not one channel.
- Autonomy is framed explicitly: **Level 1 in the MVP**, Level 2 designed but not shipped, Level 3 out.
- The north star becomes **Completed Distribution Cycles**, not diagnoses or drafts.
- "Autonomous distribution department" becomes the narrative; "fully autonomous" remains forbidden until Level 3 is real.

## 1. Definitive Banyan vision

A founder should be able to ship a product and delegate its distribution with the same confidence they delegate a deployment.

They provide the product, the update, the objective and the boundaries. Banyan understands what was built, who needs it, where those people are, what to say in each place, and what happened after it went out. The founder approves the direction and the exceptions. Banyan does the coordination.

This is not a tool that helps a founder do marketing faster. It is a system that **takes responsibility for the distribution work**: it proposes, the founder approves, it executes, it measures, and it comes back with the next move. The founder's involvement should shrink over time from managing activities to approving strategy and handling meaningful exceptions.

**Vision statement**

> A world where a founder can ship a product and delegate its distribution as confidently as they delegate deployment — with a system that understands the product, proposes the strategy, executes with approval, and proves what worked.

**The long arc**

1. **Today — approved execution.** Banyan runs one complete cycle; the founder approves the strategy and every asset.
2. **Next — supervised autonomy.** The founder sets rules, channels, cadence and boundaries; Banyan handles routine actions and escalates exceptions.
3. **Later — autonomous operations.** Banyan runs continuous distribution within explicit authorization, budget and policy, with audit, rate limits and human override.

The MVP must make the first stage real before the second stage is marketed.

## 2. One-sentence product definition

> **Banyan is an AI-native distribution department that turns a product and its updates into one complete, approved distribution cycle — strategy, channel-native assets, publishing, measured outcomes, and the next move.**

**Deconstructed**

- **AI-native:** the system does the research synthesis, strategy drafting, content adaptation, coordination and analysis — not a human operating a dashboard.
- **Distribution department:** it owns the function, not a single task. It decides what to distribute, why, where, how and what happened.
- **One complete cycle:** bounded, observable and repeatable — not an open-ended campaign or a content calendar.
- **Approved:** a human authorizes the strategy and the assets that go out under their name.
- **Measured outcomes:** first-party visits and signups, with limitations stated; never impressions dressed up as growth.
- **The next move:** the cycle ends with an evidence-backed proposal, not a report that sits in a folder.

**What it is not:** a scheduler, a copy generator, a video tool, a marketing automation builder, a general-purpose agent platform, or an agency.

## 3. Definitive MVP promise

> **Ship one update. Get one complete distribution cycle: strategy, three channel-native assets, your approval, real publishing, measured results, and the next move.**

This is the strongest promise the first version can genuinely deliver because it is:

- **Bounded** — one product, one objective, one cycle.
- **Demonstrable** — the founder can see the strategy, the assets, the live posts, the numbers and the next step.
- **Hard to fake** — a published URL and a tracked signup are evidence; a report is not.
- **Aligned with the vision** — Banyan does the work, the founder approves.
- **Commercially legible** — the value is a completed cycle, not a subscription to a content tool.

**Supporting promise for the long term**

> Banyan builds your product's presence in the market — one complete, approved cycle at a time.

**What the MVP promise does not claim**

- It does not claim full autonomy. It claims approved execution.
- It does not claim virality, revenue or product-market fit.
- It does not claim every channel. It claims two live channels and one prepared manual channel.
- It does not claim market intelligence it cannot source. Research remains hypothesis-labelled until a verified provider is integrated.
- It does not claim that a publication is a result. Only measured visits and signups count.

| Criterion | Why this segment |

| **Reachability** | They self-identify in public: Indie Hackers, r/SaaS, Hacker News, LinkedIn build-in-public, X, founder communities and accelerators. |

| **Recurring need** | Distribution is weekly by nature. The second cycle has a natural reason to exist. |
| **Feedback quality** | Technical founders are precise about generic output; they will tell the product when it is not product-specific. |

**Portrait**

- 25–45, engineer or product background, ships weekly, one to three products, comfortable with OAuth and APIs.
- Uses Stripe, Linear, Notion, Vercel and GitHub; reads Hacker News and founder newsletters; posts on LinkedIn or X inconsistently.
- Has tried ChatGPT, a scheduler, or a freelancer. None became a system.
- Trigger: a launch, the first ten customers, a plateau, a competitor out-marketing them, or an investor asking about traction.
- Objection: *"AI content is generic, and I don't want it posting without me."* The MVP must answer both before asking for money.
- Willingness-to-pay hypothesis: **$29–79/month** if one cycle saves four or more hours and produces at least one attributable conversation or signup.

**Who waits**

| Segment | Why not now |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-launch indie hackers | Nothing live to distribute; highest price sensitivity. |
| Agencies and consultants | Need multi-client workspaces, white-label reports and volume support. |
| Creators and newsletter writers | Monetize attention, not a product; different loop and metric. |
| Enterprise marketing teams | Procurement, SSO, CRM and governance would reshape the roadmap. |
| DTC and e-commerce | The primary loop is paid creative and ROAS, not text-led B2B distribution. |

**Expansion path, not MVP:** apps, APIs, open-source projects and digital products are part of the long-term vision. They enter only after the B2B SaaS cycle retains a hundred workspaces.

## 7. Complete user journey

The journey is one cycle. It starts with an update and ends with a proposed next cycle — not with a report.

| # | Stage | The founder does | Banyan does | Artifact | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Ship / intake** | Provides a product URL, a launch note, a feature update, or a repository link. | Fetches what is public, extracts the offer, audience, proof, pricing and update, and labels each fact by provenance. | Product snapshot | Founder corrects anything wrong |
| 2 | **Understand** | Reviews the snapshot and answers one question: what should this cycle produce? | Proposes the objective, metric, baseline and audience. | Objective + product snapshot | Founder chooses the objective |
| 3 | **Strategize** | Reviews and edits the positioning and the three channel bets. | Explains the reasoning, confidence and channel rationale; recommends the primary channel. | Strategy brief | Founder approves the strategy |
| 4 | **Plan** | Approves the cycle plan. | Drafts the cadence, three formats, two live channels, one manual channel, dependencies and the success metric. | Cycle plan | Founder approves the plan |
| 5 | **Create** | Edits drafts if needed. | Writes the LinkedIn post, the X thread, and the short-video script with hook, scenes, on-screen text, caption and CTA; preserves every revision. | Three assets | Founder edits freely |
| 6 | **Approve** | Approves each asset. | Enforces the gate server-side, records who approved what and when, and resets approval on any edit. | Approval record | Founder has the final say |
| 7 | **Execute** | Connects accounts or chooses the manual path. | Publishes to LinkedIn and X through the adapter, prepares the video handoff, stores provider responses, schedules bounded retries, attaches tracked links. | Publications + tracked links | Founder can revoke; blocked states are visible |
| 8 | **Measure** | Nothing. | Reads first-party visits and signups, adds available provider metrics, states what is unknown, and never fills gaps with zeros. | Results report | Founder can add manual metrics |
| 9 | **Learn / propose** | Accepts, edits or dismisses the next move. | Proposes the next cycle with evidence, confidence and the one variable to change. | Recommendation + next-cycle proposal | Founder decides whether to run it |

**A concrete example.** A founder ships "API usage alerts." Banyan reads the product and the update, proposes "20 qualified signups in 14 days" for technical founders already evaluating APIs, writes a LinkedIn insight post, an X thread on the cost of silent quota failures, and a 45-second video script showing the alert in the product. The founder edits one sentence, approves all three, connects LinkedIn and X, and publishes. The video script is exported for manual recording. Seven days later Banyan reports tracked visits and signups, says what it cannot attribute, and proposes either scaling the message or changing the hook.

## 8. Autonomous distribution operating model

### The three autonomy levels

| Level | Name | Who decides strategy | Who approves assets | Who publishes | Who handles exceptions | MVP status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **Human-approved execution** | Banyan proposes; founder approves | Founder, per asset | Banyan, after approval | Founder | **Supported** |
| **2** | **Supervised autonomy** | Founder sets goals and rules; Banyan proposes within them | Banyan auto-approves routine assets within policy; founder sees a digest | Banyan | Banyan escalates defined exceptions | **P1 — not built** |
| **3** | **Autonomous operations** | Founder sets policy, budget and boundaries | Policy-based, with sampling and audit | Banyan continuously | Banyan, with human override and incident review | **Out of scope** |

**The MVP is Level 1.** That is not a limitation to hide; it is the trust-building stage. The system takes responsibility for the work while the founder keeps the decision. Level 1 already transfers the operational burden — research, drafting, adaptation, coordination, publishing and measurement — without pretending to transfer accountability.

### The operating loop

```
Update / product URL
        ↓
Product intelligence  →  editable snapshot, provenance
        ↓
Objective + strategy  →  audience, positioning, 3 channel bets
        ↓
Cycle plan            →  3 formats, 2 live + 1 manual, metric
        ↓
Production            →  channel-native assets + media brief
        ↓
Human approval        →  per-asset gate; edits reset approval
        ↓
Execution             →  publish, handoff, retries, audit
        ↓
Measurement           →  tracked visits/signups, limitations
        ↓
Learning              →  verdict + next-cycle proposal
        ↓
Next cycle
```

### Responsibility transfer

Banyan owns **coordination**: what needs to happen, in what order, with which tools, and what to do when something fails. The founder owns **authorization and judgment**: the objective, the position, the final copy, and the exceptions. The founder remains the publisher of record. That boundary is the product's trust model, not a legal footnote.

### What "autonomous" must never mean in the MVP

- It does not mean publishing without approval.
- It does not mean spending money.
- It does not mean sending DMs or engaging in communities on the founder's behalf.
- It does not mean inventing research, metrics or outcomes.
- It does not mean a swarm of agents with no audit trail.

## 9. Agent and orchestration responsibilities

The architecture is **one orchestrator with role-specific handlers**, not a company of autonomous agents. Every task has structured inputs, an assigned role, a verification rule, a retry budget and an audit record. That is what makes the system observable and safe enough to trust with distribution.

| Role | Responsibility | Inputs | Outputs | Human gate | MVP status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Orchestrator** | Owns the cycle state, dependencies, retries, recovery and audit. Decides what is ready to run next. | Plan, task states, approvals, failures | Executed cycle, status, logs | None for coordination; all consequential actions gated | **Built** |
| **Product Intelligence** | Reads the product and update; extracts offer, audience, proof, pricing, differentiators; labels provenance. | URL, launch note, repo, manual context | Editable product snapshot | Founder corrects facts | **Partial** — profile exists, URL ingestion does not |
| **Research** | Produces sharp questions, hypothesis angles, competitor hypotheses and the sources needed to settle them. | Snapshot, objective | Research brief | None; labelled hypotheses | **Partial** — no live web access |
| **Strategist** | Proposes the objective, audience, positioning, three channel bets and the rationale. | Snapshot, research, constraints | Strategy brief + cycle plan | Founder approves strategy and plan | **Partial** — strategy agent exists, not URL-grounded |
| **Creative Director** | Turns strategy into three format-specific briefs that fit the channel and voice. | Strategy, brand voice, prior learning | Content briefs | Founder edits | **Partial** |
| **Producer** | Writes the LinkedIn post, X thread, short-video script and media instructions; preserves revisions. | Briefs, snapshot, voice | Three assets + media brief | Founder edits and approves each | **Built for text; script-only video** |
| **Publisher** | Validates connections, publishes or schedules, confirms provider responses, handles retries and blocked states. | Approved assets, connections | Publications with URLs/IDs | Approval already recorded | **Built; unverified live** |
| **Analyst** | Reads real outcomes, states limitations, evaluates the hypothesis and proposes the next move. | Publications, tracked links, events, metrics | Results report + recommendation | Founder accepts/dismisses | **Built; heuristic verdicts** |
| **Human** | Sets objective and boundaries, approves strategy and assets, handles exceptions. | Proposals, diffs, evidence | Decisions and audit trail | — | **Built** |

**Design rules**

- One orchestrator, not a swarm. Roles are responsibilities inside one runtime.
- No task runs before the plan is approved; no asset publishes before its revision is approved.
- Every provider call records its raw response; every failure is classified as retryable or permanent.
- Every recommendation cites recorded evidence or explicitly says it has none.
- The system may propose more autonomy; it may not grant it to itself.

## 11. Minimum viable end-to-end distribution workflow

This is the MVP's definition of done. If this workflow cannot run with real accounts and real numbers, the promise is not met.

### The scenario

**Input:** one product URL + one update ("we shipped API usage alerts") + one objective ("20 qualified signups in 14 days") + one primary audience (technical founders evaluating APIs).

### The required output

1. **Product snapshot** — offer, audience, proof, pricing, differentiators, and the update, each labelled as observed, user-provided or inferred; every field editable.
2. **Strategy** — one positioning hypothesis, one primary audience, three channel bets with reasons and confidence, one declared metric.
3. **Cycle plan** — three assets, two live channels, one manual channel, a cadence, dependencies and an approval gate.
4. **Three assets** —
   - a LinkedIn insight post in the founder's voice;
   - an X thread with a distinct hook and structure;
   - a 45–60 second short-video script with hook, scenes, on-screen text, caption and CTA.
5. **Approval record** — a recorded human decision for each asset, with the specific revision approved; any edit resets the gate.
6. **Live publications** — LinkedIn and X published through a configured connection, each with a provider-confirmed URL; the video script exported as a copy-ready handoff and, if the founder publishes it, recorded manually with the live URL.
7. **Tracked links** — one per channel, attached to the content item and the campaign.
8. **Measured outcomes** — visits and signups recorded first-party; provider metrics if available; explicit "unmeasured" where not.
9. **Cycle report** — what shipped, what it produced, what cannot be attributed, and the one variable to change next.
10. **Next-cycle proposal** — one recommendation citing the recorded evidence, with confidence and uncertainty.

### Acceptance criteria

| Criterion | Threshold |
| --- | --- |
| Founder active time | Under 30 minutes across intake, edits and approvals |
| Time to first approved asset | Under 24 hours from intake |
| Live publications | At least two, provider-confirmed |
| Manual channel prepared | One video handoff with a complete script |
| Tracked outcome | At least one recorded visit or signup within 7 days |
| Next-cycle proposal | One, with at least one evidence item or an explicit no-evidence statement |
| Fabricated data | Zero |
| Unauthorized external action | Zero |
| Every state visible | Draft → approved → published/blocked/failed, with provider response and retry history |

### What this proves that a report cannot

- The strategy survives contact with a real channel.
- The content is genuinely adapted, not duplicated.
- The approval boundary works under real edits.
- Publishing works, or fails honestly.
- Measurement produces a real number or an honest unknown.
- The system can recommend a next move from its own evidence.
- The founder can delegate the coordination and keep the judgment.

## 12. Human approval and autonomy boundaries

### Non-negotiable boundaries

1. **No external action without a recorded authorization.** Publishing, scheduling, spending, DMs and account changes are all consequential.
2. **Approval is revision-specific.** Editing approved copy resets it to draft; a prior approval never covers new text.
3. **The founder is the publisher of record.** Banyan acts on their behalf, within their authorization.
4. **No invented outcomes.** A publication is not a result; a result requires a recorded row.
5. **No unverified integration is presented as available.** "Blocked" is honest; "success" without provider confirmation is not.
6. **No autonomy expansion by the system.** Banyan may propose a broader mandate; only the founder can grant it.

### Consequential actions and MVP permissions

| Action | MVP permission | Level 2 (P1) | Level 3 (out) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Publish an approved asset | Yes, after per-asset approval | Auto within rules and digest | Policy-based, sampled |
| Schedule an approved asset | Yes | Auto within cadence rules | Continuous |
| Publish unapproved copy | **No** | **No** | **No** — policy still requires authorization |
| Spend money / boost a post | **No** | **No** (budget out of scope) | Explicit budget only |
| Send DMs or outreach | **No** | **No** | Out of scope |
| Delete or change an account | **No** | **No** | Out of scope |
| Connect a new channel | Founder only | Founder only | Founder only |
| Expand the mandate | Founder only | Founder only | Founder only |

### Level 2 boundaries, when built (P1)

- Allowed channels, topics, formats and cadence.
- Max posts per day and per channel.
- Blackout dates and launch freeze windows.
- Brand rules: forbidden claims, required disclosures, tone constraints.
- Escalation triggers: new channel, new audience, sensitive topic, low confidence, provider failure, unusual volume.
- Weekly digest of everything auto-approved; one-click pause; full audit trail.

### Level 3, when — and only when — it is real (P2+)

Continuous cycles within explicit policy, budget and rate limits; sampling for human review; anomaly detection; incident review; human override; the ability to stop everything in one action. **Do not market Level 3 in the MVP.** The honest claim is "approved execution," not "autonomous operations."

## 14. Product differentiation

| Alternative | What it sells | What it leaves to the founder | Banyan's difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Schedulers** (Postiz, Buffer, Hootsuite, Metricool) | Publishing, calendars, analytics | Deciding what to say, why, for whom, and whether it worked | Banyan decides the strategy, creates the assets, and measures the outcome |
| **AI writers** (Jasper and similar) | Content generation with brand context | Channel choice, publishing, measurement, the next move | Banyan owns the whole cycle, not just the draft |
| **AI video tools** | Asset production | Strategy, distribution, attribution | Video is one format in a coordinated cycle, not the product |
| **Marketing automation** | Workflow builders and CRM-centric campaigns | Designing the workflow, maintaining the data, interpreting the results | Banyan ships a default distribution workflow; no builder required |
| **General-purpose agents** | A platform to assemble agents | Designing the agents, tools, prompts, guardrails and evaluation | Banyan has an opinionated distribution loop with approvals and audit built in |
| **Agencies** | Done-for-you strategy and execution | Budget, opacity, and no reusable system | Self-serve, evidence-visible, and the system stays with the founder |
| **Dashboards** | Observation and reporting | Acting on the insight | Banyan acts, then reports what happened |

**The real differentiator: responsibility transfer.** Competitors sell tools that help the founder do marketing. Banyan takes responsibility for the distribution cycle and returns only the decisions that need a human. That is a different product category, not a feature comparison.

**The proof differentiator: the completed cycle.** Drafts are easy; publications are harder; measured outcomes are hardest. Banyan's demo is a real cycle with real URLs and real numbers, not a content folder.

**The context differentiator: product intelligence plus outcome memory.** The system starts from what the product is and gets better because it remembers what worked. Schedulers do not collect that context; generic models cannot reconstruct it.

**What is real today versus the roadmap**

- Real: product profile, strategy drafts, text content, approval gate, Postiz adapter, tracked links, first-party events, agent runtime, audit.
- Partial: URL intake, live publishing, media (script only), research (hypotheses), recommendations (heuristic).
- Roadmap: Level 2 autonomy, image/video generation, external research, direct LinkedIn OAuth, benchmarks.

**The moat.** Not the model. The moat is the product-specific context graph, the cycle history, the approval and audit trail, and the outcome data. Today it is per workspace; it becomes a benchmark only after many products run cycles. The first defensible asset is the completed-cycle record. Say that honestly.

## 16. Success metrics and proof of value

**North star: Completed Distribution Cycles per active workspace per month.**

A cycle counts only when one workspace has, inside the cycle window:

- one approved strategy and one declared objective;
- at least two published assets (provider-confirmed or manually recorded with a live URL);
- at least one tracked link;
- at least one measured visit or signup, or an explicit "unmeasured" with a reason;
- a next-cycle proposal delivered to the founder.

This single number captures the promise: strategy, execution, evidence and learning. It cannot be inflated by drafts, impressions or agent activity.

**Proof metrics**

| Metric | What it proves | Target for the first cohort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cycle completion rate | The workflow survives real life | 8 of 10 design partners complete one |
| Time to first approved asset | The system is fast enough to use | Under 24 hours |
| Founder active time | The burden really moved | Under 30 minutes per cycle |
| Publish success rate | Execution works | Above 95% for configured connections |
| Approval rate / edit rate | Output quality and trust | Approval above 50%; edit rate trending down |
| Measured outcomes | The evidence layer works | At least 5 of 10 cycles show a tracked visit or signup |
| Next-cycle acceptance | The learning is useful | At least half accepted or edited, not dismissed |
| D30 return | The loop retains | Above 30% of activated workspaces |

**Business metrics**

- Free intake → saved workspace → first cycle → paid conversion.
- MRR, churn, expansion, annual prepay.
- CAC and payback by channel.
- Gross margin per plan, with inference and hosting measured.
- Support minutes per workspace — the real cost of the supervised stage.

**Quality guardrails**

- Zero fabricated metrics or market facts.
- Zero unauthorized external actions.
- Every blocked publication names the missing setup.
- Every recommendation shows confidence and evidence, or says it has none.
- Every publication stores the provider response and retry history.
- URL intake p95 under 90 seconds, with an honest failure state.

**Anti-metrics, never reported as success:** drafts generated, words written, agents running, tasks completed, impressions, likes, followers, "automation rate."

**The proof narrative for the launch:** *One product. One objective. Three assets. Two live channels. Real URLs. Real numbers. A next move.* That is more convincing than any dashboard.
